27.3b Ticketing boundary: how much do we build without becoming a ticketing system?

Question: if we build internal tickets, aren't we drifting from the project's main path? Can we say "if you have a ticketing system we'll integrate; if you don't, that's your problem"?

Answer: we should not build a ticketing system. But "that's your problem" doesn't work either. The right statement is: "We are not a ticketing system. We record the request, deliver it to the owner, and the employee can track it. If you have a ticketing system or office automation, we integrate with it." That is, we build only about 5% of a ticketing system: capture, handoff and tracking.

Why "that's your problem" doesn't work

  1. The promise is made by our IVA, not the customer's system. If an employee's request gets lost, they say "this line is useless" and stop calling. Employee participation is exactly what our product sells.
  2. Most organizations in Iran have no HR ticketing system. They usually have office automation and work inboxes (Chargoon Didgah, Rahkaran, Hamkaran System), not Jira or ServiceNow. So "integration only" means most Iranian customers get no tracking. INFERENCE Needs to be checked with the market.
  3. Sensitive topics must not be sent outside. A harassment case or medical matter must not go into a general work inbox or the IT queue. A small internal queue is needed in any case.
  4. The closest international competitor has an internal desk. Leena AI, like us, is an assistant layer, not an HR system. Even so, it has a light internal desk and also integrates with ServiceNow, Jira and Zendesk. Moveworks only integrates with ServiceNow, because it sells to companies that all have ServiceNow (and it is now part of ServiceNow itself). WEB W-LE1, W-LE2, W-MW2, G11

27.3e Time-bound announcements: "why is the parking lot closed today?"

Need: some problems are temporary: the parking lot is closed, the cafeteria isn't open today, the elevator is broken. On such days dozens of people call and ask the same question. The reason may also differ each day: today the parking lot is closed for repairs, tomorrow for an event. So when something happens or is about to happen, the bot's knowledge must be updated at that moment, and the bot must tell anyone who asks the reason for that day.

Example: at 7 a.m. the facilities manager writes in the panel: "The central building's parking lot is closed today until 14:00 for gate repairs. Alternative parking: the side street." End: today at 14:00.
At 9 an employee calls and asks "why is the parking lot closed?" The IVA answers: "According to today's 7 a.m. announcement, the central building's parking lot is closed until 14:00 for gate repairs. You can use the side-street parking."
At 14:00 the announcement is removed automatically and from then on the bot gives the normal answer again.

What Phoenix has today: most of the groundwork is built. Every Q&A in the knowledge base has a start and end time. A background job every 5 minutes (default) adds answers whose time has come to the bot's search and removes expired ones. So this example already works today, with at most 5 minutes' delay. 27.4 Nine key judgments

  1. "Answering" is no longer an advantage. All 13 major HCMs have an HR service agent (§25.5b). None has Persian voice over the phone with an in-country model. Competition is on channel, language and trust.
  2. Trust first, then features. Most security tasks are small (S or M). Without them the procurement review fails, however good the AI is.
  3. The org tree is a hidden dependency. Access scope, minimum group size, ticket routing, personalization and per-unit analysis all need it. Build it first.
  4. Tagging = fixed categories + automatic discovery + human correction. Qualtrics, Google and AWS use this combination. Fixed categories alone miss new problems; automatic discovery alone makes the dashboard unstable. WEB W-QT1, W-GO1, W-AWS2
  5. Logging unanswered questions is the cheapest high-value task. It's a few days' work, improves the bot every week and produces a tangible report for EX. Moveworks has turned exactly this into a "write this article" suggestion. WEB W-MW3
  6. Tickets first need a contractual decision (section 27.3), not code.
  7. Pay and promotion are not tickets. A "I want a raise" queue creates expectations HR can't meet, and the blame falls on the product.
  8. If the loop isn't closed, participation dies. Action plans and "you said, we did" messages are cheap and are the reason employees call again.
  9. Take back ownership of the analysis engine. The tags HR judges the product by come from coa-ai, which hasn't been touched in 14 months and was built for a customer contact center. This is a P0 risk, not an optional rewrite.

Glossary

Term Meaning
IDOR — Insecure Direct Object Reference Insecure Direct Object Reference — a class of access-control vulnerability; the server takes an identifier (such as call_id or a file name) from the user and fetches it directly without checking that the record actually belongs to that user/their access. Example in this document: GET /api/v1/files?name=<call-id>.wav — by changing the call-id, other people's call audio files can be downloaded.
VAD — Voice Activity Detection Voice Activity Detection — an algorithm (usually a lightweight model) that runs in real time on the audio stream and separates frames containing speech from silence/background noise. VAD's output is the input to Turn Detection, and in the voice-agent architecture (LiveKit) it runs before ASR.
Turn Detection / Endpointing Logic that, based on VAD output (and sometimes linguistic signals), decides the user has finished their turn and it is now the agent's turn to respond. Detecting too early cuts the user off mid-sentence; detecting too late causes a noticeable delay in the agent's response; tuning it is a direct trade-off on the conversation experience.
Barge-in A capability that lets the user speak while the agent is talking (TTS) and stop the agent — the natural behavior of human conversation. In this project's code it is controlled by flags such as allow_interruptions in chatbot-iva-agent/src/agents/agentlib.py and entrypoint.py (e.g. in special states such as the welcome message or user silence, interruption is disabled).
Egress In LiveKit, the process of exporting/recording a Room's content (real-time audio/video) to an external file or stream (such as saving to object storage). In this project it is used to record the user's and agent's audio tracks (participant_track_path, agent_track_path) so they are available later for offline ASR or audit.
RAG — Retrieval-Augmented Generation Retrieval-Augmented Generation — a pattern where, before calling the LLM, relevant documents are retrieved from a knowledge base (vector DB / knowledge base) and added to the prompt as context, so the answer is grounded in the organization's actual data rather than only the model's pre-trained knowledge. Reducing hallucination and updating knowledge without fine-tuning are its main benefits.
ReAct An agent design pattern (Reason + Act) in which the model iterates between "reasoning about the next step" (Reason), "running a tool/action" (Act) and "observing the result" (Observe) until it reaches a final answer; the basis of many agentic frameworks, including the tool-calling structure in this project.
SSE — Server-Sent Events Server-Sent Events — a one-way HTTP protocol (server to client) that pushes text events one after another over an open connection; in this project it is used to stream the LLM response incrementally (token by token) to the chat client, without needing a full WebSocket.
DLQ — Dead Letter Queue Dead Letter Queue — a separate queue (on Kafka/RabbitMQ) that holds messages whose processing has failed repeatedly and that the main consumer could not complete; instead of the message being lost or retried endlessly, it allows manual review/replay later.
Circuit Breaker A service resilience pattern: when calls to an external dependency (such as the ASR service) keep failing, the circuit breaker temporarily "opens" the call path so new requests fail immediately and don't put pressure on the broken service; after a time window it tests again (Half-Open) and closes the path if successful. It is implemented in golang/grogu/internal/circuitbreak and used in golang/grogu/internal/asr/service.go.
MCP — Model Context Protocol Model Context Protocol — an open standard for connecting language models to external tools/data sources in a uniform way (the MCP server declares capabilities, the model/agent calls them), instead of writing each integration ad hoc and custom. In this project agentlib-plugin-mcp plays this role.
WER — Word Error Rate Word Error Rate — the standard measure of ASR accuracy: the sum of substituted, deleted and inserted words in the transcribed text divided by the total number of words in the reference text. Lower WER means a more accurate transcript; it complements the transcript_confidence metric stored per utterance in this project's schema.
Service Number (سرشماره) A short/special number the customer dials to reach a service. Allocating it is only an entry identifier; until routing, connectivity, the SBC and the SIP trunk are ready, the service cannot be used.
Zone / Core Network (the operator's core network) The central part of the operator's network that controls and routes calls; it decides which trunk/destination a call to a service number goes to.
Signaling Control messages for setting up, changing and ending a call (who, to where, free or not, connected or dropped). Separate from the audio itself (media).
Backbone The operator's main high-capacity transport network (fiber + core equipment) that connects zones and data centers. "Connecting to the backbone" means connectivity + routing + IP + security, not just a cable.
VLAN / ACL / QoS — Virtual Local Area Network / Access Control List / Quality of Service VLAN: logical network separation on one physical infrastructure. ACL: allow/deny list for traffic by IP/port. QoS: prioritizing voice traffic (e.g. DSCP EF) to reduce jitter and loss.
SBC — Session Border Controller Session Border Controller — the security and control gateway for SIP calls at the network edge: session control, security, NAT traversal, topology hiding, capacity limiting and message normalization. The operator's SIP trunk usually terminates on it.
SIP — Session Initiation Protocol Session Initiation Protocol (RFC 3261) — the signaling protocol for voice calls over IP, with messages such as INVITE, 180 Ringing, 200 OK, ACK, BYE, OPTIONS and MESSAGE. It does not carry the audio itself.
SIP Trunk (Session Initiation Protocol Trunk) A logical SIP connection between two systems (e.g. the operator and the SBC) with agreed peer IPs, transport, concurrent channel cap and number format.
RTP — Real-time Transport Protocol Real-time Transport Protocol — carries the audio itself (media) over UDP, usually in 20 ms packets. Its path may differ from the SIP path.
SDP — Session Description Protocol Session Description Protocol — the body of SIP messages that negotiates the IP, port and codecs for media (offer in INVITE, answer in 200 OK). A wrong IP in SDP is a common cause of "no audio."
Codec (G.711 — ITU-T standard; PCMA/PCMU) Audio encoding algorithm. G.711 (PCMA/PCMU) is the common telephony standard: 8 kHz, 64 kbps net (~87 kbps with overhead). STT in the IVA works with this narrowband audio.
Jitter / Packet Loss (delay variation / lost packets) Jitter: variation in the arrival time of RTP packets; packet loss: lost packets. Both cause choppy/robotic audio and lower STT accuracy.
NAT Traversal — Network Address Translation Traversal The problem of SIP/RTP crossing NAT: the internal address written in SDP/Via is not reachable from outside; the result is one-way audio or the call dropping after ~32 seconds. The SBC usually solves it.
LiveKit SIP / Dispatch The LiveKit bridge that turns a SIP call into a Room and makes the caller a participant with identity sip_+98…; the dispatch rule decides which agent worker takes the call. SIP headers appear as sip.h.* in the participant's attributes.
SIP MESSAGE (Redirect) — Session Initiation Protocol MESSAGE Phoenix's method for transferring to a human agent: instead of standard REFER, sip-messager sends a MESSAGE with X-Resolution and X-Destination (default 6040) to the hop extracted from Via and expects a 202.
HA / Failover — High Availability High Availability: multiple nodes/data centers so that one failing doesn't stop the service. Failover: automatic transfer of traffic to a healthy node.
Concurrent Calls The number of simultaneous calls — the main unit of capacity: each concurrent call consumes one SIP session, two RTP streams, one agent job and one concurrent STT/LLM/TTS session.
Fine-tuning Retraining the parameters of a pre-trained model on a specific dataset so it better learns a behavior, tone or particular task. Unlike RAG, it is not suited to changing knowledge (prices, procedures). RAG and fine-tuning can be combined: RAG provides the knowledge, fine-tuning shapes the behavior. Phoenix has no fine-tuning code.
k-anonymity A statistical privacy model: data or aggregate statistics are shown only when at least k records/people stand behind that group, so that a specific person cannot be identified within the group by combining filters (e.g. preventing "negative sentiment" from being shown for a group of only 2 people, which effectively reveals identity). In this document it is used as the mechanism for suppressing small groups (PRV-05).
